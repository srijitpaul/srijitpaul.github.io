# Editing the site

The site is static HTML: no Ruby, Jekyll build, server-side code, or package installation is needed. `.nojekyll` tells GitHub Pages to serve these files directly. The `main` branch contains only this site. The 2018 Hydejack site is kept in the local `master` branch and is not published; its files may still be on disk, but `.gitignore` excludes them.

- Edit `index.html` for the home page and `assets/css/scholar.css` for styling.
- Headings use the locally hosted Latin subset of Rozha One, released by Indian Type Foundry and designed for Latin by Shiva Nallaperumal. Its SIL Open Font License is in `assets/fonts/rozha-one/OFL.txt`; the webfont is from Google Fonts.
- The CV link points to `assets/CV.pdf`, a public build of the CV without the phone number, nationality, date of birth or referees' contact details. Do not copy `cv_only.pdf` or `main.pdf` from the CV source folder here directly: they contain those details. To make a public version, copy the CV source to a temporary folder, delete the phone and nationality/date-of-birth `\makefield` lines and the `\input{referee-full}` line from `main.tex`, and build the `cv` mode under a separate job name (pdflatex, biber, then pdflatex twice, as the Makefile does). Before committing, check it with `pdftotext assets/CV.pdf - | grep -E '\+[0-9]{1,3}[- ][0-9]|[0-9]{1,2} [A-Z][a-z]+ 19[0-9]{2}|References'`. It must print nothing; on the full CV it flags all three fields.
- To add a Hadronicle article, copy `hadronicle/what-is-a-hadron/index.html` into a new directory under `hadronicle/`, edit its title, description and article text, add a date if desired, and add a link to it in `hadronicle/index.html`. The included note is an example, not a published article.
- The Bengali science section is `mogojastro/index.html`. To add an article, copy `mogojastro/hadron/index.html` into a new directory under `mogojastro/`, replace the example text and metadata, and link it from the section index. Its sample note is not a published article.
- Write display math as `$$ ... $$` or `\[ ... \]`, and inline math as `\( ... \)`. Escape HTML characters such as `<` and `&` in article text and formulas as `&lt;` and `&amp;`. The local KaTeX renderer supports a subset of LaTeX; check the rendered result.

KaTeX 0.19.1 is locally hosted in `assets/vendor/katex/` with its license. Keep it updated when security fixes are released. Preview the site from the repository root with `python3 -m http.server 8000`, then open `http://localhost:8000/`. Do not open the HTML files directly, since links are rooted at the site URL.

The Research section is visual first. It has a two-sentence lead, a closed card with the lattice figure (`details.tool-card`; the tool text is in a nested note), and seven research pillars. Each pillar card shows only an icon, its title, the big question and one visual. The visuals are the HVP animation (pillar 2), the Δ and ρ animations (pillars 4 and 6), and four schematic drawings (`figure.pillar-visual`): dark matter scattering off a nucleus, B̄⁰ → ρ⁺ℓ⁻ν̄ with ρ⁺ → π⁺π⁰, the quark content of T_cc and the H dibaryon, and an autoencoder for Ising spins. "Read more" opens short "The physics", "My approach" and "Key works" sections, plus the remaining figures. Long figure captions are shortened to one line, and their full text, with every caveat, sits in a collapsed "About this figure" note (`details.fig-notes`). When editing, keep the visible caption short and put caveats in the note. The text was corrected against the sources: 85% of the *matter* is dark; 99.9% of the mass of *ordinary* matter is in nucleons; hadron lifetimes are a few × 10⁻²⁴ s; SM CP violation is "generally believed" too small; N → Δ is at m_π ≈ 255 MeV. The tool note keeps the scoped anomaly sentence (Lüscher 1998) and the Yang–Mills sentence (Scientific American, November 2026). `tests/research-structure.test.js` checks the pillar order, that each card has a visual before "Read more", the short captions with their notes, the scoping, the preprint labels and the sources, and that the draft's overstatements do not return.

The lattice figure, in the tool card, is a grid of eight time slices of four by four sites, drawn in perspective with one space direction left out. Its four steps show the grid with spacing a and size L; quark fields on sites, gluon links and a plaquette; a correlation function, from a point source to a time slice summed over space, joined by symbolic quark propagators; and the continuum limit. The continuum-limit step draws the same 0.4 fm square at the window paper's coarsest and finest spacings, 0.099 and 0.039 fm. The scattering captions say how the lattice actually obtains each amplitude: from finite-volume energy levels using Lüscher's formalism (Δ and ρ), or finite-volume matrix elements with the Lellouch–Lüscher factor (πγ → ππ). The lattice details come from each paper's arXiv abstract. In the window figure, step 1 marks the lattice times t = na for a ≈ 0.099 fm. Both stepped figures use the shared `stepped-figure`, `step-controls`, `step-play` and `step-text` classes. `data-default-step` sets the step shown first. `tests/lattice-figure.test.js` checks the grid and the quoted lattice details.

The window paper's entry begins with the leading-order hadronic vacuum polarization (HVP) diagram. A muon emits a virtual photon, which turns into a blob of hadrons; the blob turns back into a virtual photon, which the muon reabsorbs. An external-field photon attaches to the muon line between the two vertices, as in any g − 2 vertex diagram. The blob stands for the sum over all hadronic states. It is drawn as a quark loop with gluons only as a symbol for the lattice QCD calculation. The animation traces the diagram in reading order. Its caption says this is not a history in time: a Feynman diagram includes all time orderings, and the virtual photon and hadrons are not observed particles. Each "Play animation" button stops its animation after `data-duration` milliseconds (default 6000), so keep that value equal to the CSS keyframe durations. `tests/hvp-figure.test.js` checks this, the diagram's topology and the caption's caveats.

The window-observable infographic follows [Cè et al., arXiv:2206.06582v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06582). Its four steps show the smoothed step functions Θ(t, t₀, Δ) and Θ(t, t₁, Δ) (Eqs. 7–8), their difference, the intermediate-distance weight (Eq. 5), and all three weights (Eqs. 4–6). It ends with the paper's reasons for the intermediate window (Sect. II). The weights are drawn as separate curves rather than stacked areas, so that each curve's height is its value; a dashed line shows that the three weights add up to 1. The steps only fade between these exactly defined functions. Nothing is drawn or swept along t, since that would suggest something evolving in time, and Euclidean time is not real time. The plot shows weights only, not the correlator G(t) or the integrand. The result chart shows three values of a_μ^win, all in units of 10⁻¹⁰. The first is the paper's result including isospin-breaking corrections, 237.30 ± 1.46 (Eq. 45). The second is the 2022 data-driven evaluation of Colangelo et al., 229.4 ± 1.4, which the paper quotes as a 3.9σ difference. The third is the CMD-3-based data-driven value of Davier et al., Eur. Phys. J. C 84 (2024), arXiv:2312.02053v2, Fig. 12: 234.2 ± 1.2 (π⁺π⁻) ± 1.0 (other channels), drawn with the two uncertainties combined in quadrature. It uses CMD-3's π⁺π⁻ data, extended outside CMD-3's energy range and complemented by other channels from combined data. BaBar's 2026 π⁺π⁻ measurement (arXiv:2611.04621, arXiv:2611.04596) gives only energy-range contributions to a_μ^hvp, not an intermediate-window value, so it is not shown. Add it only when a published window evaluation exists. On the smoothing: RBC/UKQCD (arXiv:1801.07224), who introduced the windows, write that the tanh smearing helps control discretization errors, and that Δ = 0.15 fm gives a sufficiently sharp transition without noticeably increasing them. `tests/window-figure.test.js` recomputes every plotted point and the result-chart positions from these formulas and numbers. Run it after editing the figure.

The scattering animations illustrate resonant contributions, not measured trajectories or the full amplitude. Each moving hadron uses one representative valence flavor/color term from the shared library with a symbolic gluon field, not a full color-singlet state. The Δ illustration selects the blue π⁺ term and the red-green-blue proton and Δ terms: a blue d and anti-blue d̄ annihilate into gluonic fields, leaving three differently colored u quarks; the reverse illustrates pair creation. This is one possible contribution, not a literal trajectory, a complete QCD amplitude, or a mechanism determined by the isospin coefficients. The other scenes use the first term; neutral mesons show only their uū component. The πγ\* scene uses a wavy line as a conventional symbol for a virtual photon, not a literal free electromagnetic wave. The full superpositions are available below each figure. The captions give isospin Clebsch–Gordan overlaps in the isospin limit: π⁺p is pure I = 3/2; the displayed I = 1 two-pion states have coefficients ±1/√2 in ordered isospin slots. For the ππ P-wave, the antisymmetric isospin factor is combined with an odd spatial wavefunction. These squared overlaps are not resonance-formation or decay probabilities. The πγ\* caption only decomposes the final ππ state, not the electromagnetic current.

Each scattering figure has optional questions and individually revealed answers using native HTML disclosures, with no JavaScript required. The πγ\* G-parity question applies to the hadronic current in the isospin limit: the G-odd isoscalar current can connect the G-odd pion to the G-even two-pion state, whereas the G-even isovector current cannot. The electromagnetic interaction as a whole is not assumed to conserve G-parity.

`assets/js/qcd-hadrons.js` contains the shared quark and hadron diagrams. To reuse them, load the script and put space-separated hadron IDs in a `data-qcd-hadrons` container, with a text fallback. Each illustrated ket is a term in the color-singlet valence structure, not a separate hadron. The circles and positions are symbolic, not physical boundaries or positions; the waves evoke gluonic fields, not individual gluon paths. The diagrams are not full QCD wavefunctions. Neutral flavor combinations assume isospin symmetry, and the ordered baryon slots show only the antisymmetric color tensor. Antiquarks are outlined and use the same hue as the corresponding color, with bars on their flavor and anticolor labels. Keep these limitations visible when adding diagrams.

`energy/index.html` shows a live estimate of the energy used by the current visitor's browsing in one tab. `assets/js/visit-energy.js` is loaded on every page. On each page it records the bytes received over the network (Resource Timing `transferSize`) and the time the page is visible. It keeps the totals for the tab in `sessionStorage`, and nothing is sent anywhere. The energy page multiplies the bytes by the Sustainable Web Design Model v4 operational intensities for data centres (0.055 kWh/GB) and networks (0.059 kWh/GB). These are attributional averages, not marginal values. It multiplies the visible time by a device power that the visitor can change. The presets, 1.4 W for a phone and 10 W for a laptop, come from the measured examples cited on the page. Every new page must load this script and allow `script-src 'self'` in its CSP; `tests/visit-energy.test.js` checks both. The earlier battery measurement of the author's laptop (`assets/data/energy-2026-11-12.csv`) is no longer published; the file is kept locally only. Keep the site light, since its weight now appears in every visitor's estimate. The favicon was reduced from a 299 kB 507-pixel PNG to a 3 kB 32-pixel one for this reason.

## Security and publishing

- **Allowlist.** `.gitignore` ignores everything except the files this site needs. When you add a page or asset, add a matching `!` rule, then run `git status --ignored` and confirm that only intended files are staged. The repository is public, so anything committed is public, even if no page links to it.
- **Content Security Policy.** Each page sets a CSP in a `<meta>` tag: no third-party requests, no inline scripts or inline styles, no forms, plugins or `<base>` changes. Pages with JavaScript allow only scripts from this site; the others allow none. Keep all scripts, styles and fonts in separate files under `assets/`, and do not add `style="…"` attributes or inline `<script>` blocks. A new page should copy the CSP from a similar existing page.
- **Limits of GitHub Pages.** Pages does not let sites set HTTP response headers. As checked on 16 November 2026, it sends no `Content-Security-Policy`, `X-Frame-Options` or `X-Content-Type-Options` header, and a `<meta>` CSP cannot use `frame-ancestors`, so other sites can embed these pages in frames. The site has no forms or logins, so framing it gains an attacker little. Plain-HTTP requests are redirected to HTTPS.
- **Repository settings.** In GitHub, under Settings → Pages, publish from `main` / root and enable "Enforce HTTPS". Use two-factor authentication on the GitHub account.
- **Pushing.** Push only the `main` branch (`git push origin main`). Never use `git push --all` or `--mirror`: local branches and `refs/agents/…` checkpoints contain older files, including unredacted CVs.

## Colours and colour blindness

The figures use three categorical colours: red-orange `#d77343`, mint `#b2f6d7` and periwinkle `#8085c8`. They serve as the quark colour charges red, green and blue, the SD, ID and LD windows, the result-chart rows and the HVP diagram's lines. Under simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia (Machado et al. 2009), every pair differs by a CIEDE2000 colour difference of at least 27. The earlier palette fell as low as 3, making red and green indistinguishable for red-green colour-blind visitors. `tests/palette.test.js` checks the palette and fails if the old colours return. Colour is also never the only cue. Figures label their curves and rows directly, quark circles carry r, g or b subscripts in the colour diagrams, and photons and gluons differ in shape as well as colour. The red-orange and the periwinkle have similar lightness, so a visitor with no colour vision at all (achromatopsia, very rare) relies on these labels.
